# Shelter Photography Proposal

2026-09-20 · @Someone

## What I am offering

Free photography for every animal that comes through your doors, every week, for a year. No cost, no catch, no invoice at any point.

Edited images delivered within 48 hours of each visit, sized for your website, your adoption listings, and your social media. You own them outright and use them however you want, with no credit required and no restrictions.

Good photographs get animals adopted faster. Most shelters know this and almost none can afford a photographer on an ongoing basis. That is the gap I am offering to fill.

## What I am asking for

Permission to photograph a documentary project alongside the adoption work.

I want to follow a small number of animals over the course of a year, from the day they arrive to whatever happens next, and publish the result as a book and a series of written pieces. Not a promotional piece about your shelter. An honest record of what a year inside one looks like.

That means following the outcomes that are hard, not only the adoptions. Animals that sit for months. Animals that get returned. Animals that do not make it. I am telling you this at the start rather than discovering it halfway through, because it is the part you have every reason to be cautious about and because a version that only shows the happy endings is not worth making.

What I think it is worth to you: the public understands shelters through adoption photos, which show the good day and nothing around it. Very little exists that shows the work honestly, including how hard the decisions are and how much care goes into them. That story is currently told mostly by people who are angry at shelters rather than by anyone who has spent a year inside one.

## How it would work

One visit a week, same day, same time, whatever slot works best for your staff. Consistency matters more than convenience for me, because animals and people both photograph differently once someone is familiar rather than novel.

On site I work around you, never the other way around. No lighting rigs, no staging, no asking anyone to hold anything or stand anywhere. If a visit needs to be cut short or skipped, that is a normal week, not a problem.

I will follow whatever protocols you have for volunteers, including any training you require, and I am happy to be treated as one. If it helps, I will do volunteer shifts as well. I would rather be a familiar face than a visitor with a camera.

Every adoption photo goes to you regardless of how the documentary side develops. If you decide six months in that you want the project to stop, the photography continues anyway for the rest of the year.

## Your control over the project

These are my opening terms. I expect you will want to change some of them, and I would rather negotiate now than discover a disagreement in month nine.

| Situation | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Any given day | Any staff member can say not today, for any reason or none, and I stop |
| Any animal | You can withdraw an animal from the project at any point before publication |
| Euthanasia cases | Written about, yes. Photographed only where you agree case by case. The procedure itself, never |
| Staff and volunteers | Nobody appears or is named without their own agreement, separate from yours |
| Before anything publishes | You see it first, with enough time to raise objections |

One thing worth naming now rather than later: name a single person on your side who can settle a disagreement about what publishes. If that person exists from the start, a difficult conversation in month nine stays a conversation.

What I am not offering is approval over the story itself. If the year shows something uncomfortable about how the system works, that goes in. What I am offering is that nothing about your shelter or your people gets published that you have not seen first and had the chance to argue about.

## What I am asking you to say yes to

Not the year. Just a month.

Four visits, free photography from day one, and no commitment to the documentary project at all. At the end of the month you will have a month of adoption photos and a clear sense of whether having me around is useful or irritating. Then we talk about the rest.

If the answer after four weeks is that you want the photographs but not the project, I will take that deal and keep shooting.

The only thing I need to start is a day of the week and someone to tell me where not to go.
